# FINAL ABSTRACT

Automated feature engineering systems are highly effective at algorithmically expanding a dataset's feature space, but evaluating every generated candidate transformation quickly becomes computationally intractable. To mitigate this search cost, CAFET (Continual Automated Feature Engineering via Transfer) investigates whether tracking past candidate evaluations can optimize the exploration of new feature spaces under strict computational budgets. We implement a modular end-to-end framework featuring dataset profiling, automated candidate generation, an experience repository, and budget-aware search. Through this architecture, CAFET tracks historical utility to explore cross-dataset utility prediction. In a series of progressive empirical experiments across ten distinct datasets, we evaluate whether adaptive acquisition driven by unsupervised structural representations can consistently outperform random exploration. Our findings demonstrate that predicting expected feature utility out-of-sample using target-free structural embeddings is unreliable. Under strict cross-validated and independent evaluation protocols, the investigated adaptive search methods did not establish a reliable improvement over a simple random baseline. These empirical results clarify the limitations of purely structural context vectors in cross-dataset transfer, emphasizing that the functional AutoFE infrastructure and experience accumulation provided by CAFET form a critical foundation for future research in target-aware feature meta-learning.
